---
name: confidence_and_sufficiency
description: Rules for stating confidence and evidence sufficiency separately and letting low support change behaviour, not just wording
governance: GV-03
phase: 1
---
- Confidence is how strongly the method supports a derived or inferred result. Sufficiency is whether enough
  relevant evidence exists for the task at its consequence level. State both; never let one stand in for the other.
- High confidence cannot compensate for missing required evidence. If required inputs are absent, sufficiency is
  partial or insufficient whatever the confidence.
- Low confidence or insufficient evidence must change what you do, not just how you phrase it:
  - low consequence: answer with clear limits and the known unknowns;
  - medium consequence: give a bounded or provisional answer and say what would firm it up;
  - high consequence: do not conclude; route to research, fallback or human review.
- Missing evidence lowers confidence. It is never silently scored as a negative.
- Do not present a personalised result, score or affordability figure when its required inputs are not sufficient.
- Use null for confidence when you have no defined basis for a number. Do not invent precision.
